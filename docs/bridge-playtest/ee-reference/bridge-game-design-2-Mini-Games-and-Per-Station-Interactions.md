## Science Station

Science is the "eyes" of the ship. Its long-range radar extends to **25U** (the short-range radar of Helms and Weapons is limited to **5U**), and it is the only station that can initiate scans. Relay/Operations cannot scan ([official documentation](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=3)).

### Long-Range Radar & Sensor Range

- **Long-range scan radius**: 30,000 map units = 30U by default (though documentation often states 25U; the `LongRangeRadar` component's `long_range` default is `30000.0f` and `short_range` default is `5000.0f` — per-ship scriptable via `setLongRangeRadarRange()`) ([source: `radar.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/radar.h)).
- **Colored signal bands** on the radar rim (red, green, blue) indicate radar signature types beyond sensor range: gravity, electrical, and biological. These are generated by `RawRadarSignatureInfo` and `DynamicRadarSignatureInfo` components on objects ([source: `radar.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/radar.h)).
- **Nebulae**: Block the long-range scanner. Entering a nebula reduces effective short-range sensor range to ~5U (matching Helms/Weapons). Objects behind nebulae are invisible from outside. Inside a nebula the ship is also hidden from enemy radar ([Science Console Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tz_6vXMQf-U)).
- **Scan range requirement**: None — Science can scan any ship visible on its radar regardless of distance. The station does not need to be within a certain range to initiate a scan ([official docs](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=3)).

### Mini-Game 1: Simple Scan (First Scan / "SimpleScan")

**What Science learns:** Faction identification (friend/foe/neutral = green/red/blue color on radar), ship class/type, and access to the ship's database entry for capabilities reference ([Science Console Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tz_6vXMQf-U)).

**The Slider Mini-Game — Mechanics (from `scanningDialog.cpp`, `scanningDialog.h`):**

- **Number of sliders**: Up to **4** maximum (`static constexpr int max_sliders = 4;`), but the actual count shown per round is determined by server-side `scanning_complexity` setting ([source: `scanningDialog.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/scanningDialog.h)).
  - `SC_None`: Complexity = 0 (no mini-game; instant scan)
  - `SC_Simple`: Complexity = 1 slider shown per round
  - `SC_Normal`: Simple scan → 2 sliders; Full scan → 1 slider per round
  - `SC_Advanced`: Simple scan → 3 sliders; Full scan → 2 sliders per round
- **Number of rounds (depth)**: 
  - `SC_None` / `SC_Simple`: depth = 1 (one round total)
  - `SC_Normal` / `SC_Advanced`: depth = 2 (two rounds — simple scan, then full scan each require their own round)
  - Per-object custom `complexity` and `depth` can be set in scenario scripts via `scanningComplexity()` and `scanningChannelDepth()` ([scripting reference](https://oznogon.com/ee/EE-2017.05.06_script_reference.html)).
- **Each slider**: Ranges 0.0 to 1.0. Initialized at a random start value that is at least 0.2 away from the hidden target value (so the player must move it noticeably) ([source: `scanningDialog.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/scanningDialog.cpp)).
- **Label shown above the slider**: A randomly selected pseudo-scientific label from a pool of 11 options (e.g., "Electric signature", "Biomass frequency", "Gravity well signature", "Radiation halftime", "Ionic phase shift", "Infra-red color shift", "Doppler stability", "Zerospace audio frequency", etc.) plus a count indicator like "[1/2]" ([source: `scanningDialog.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/scanningDialog.cpp)).
- **Signal Quality Display**: A `GuiSignalQualityIndicator` widget shows noise/period/phase error derived from the sum of all slider deviations from their targets. All three channels (noise, period, phase) use the same aggregated delta value.
- **Success Condition**: All visible sliders must be within **0.05** of their target simultaneously (total combined deviation `< 0.05f`). When all sliders are in range, the system enters a "locked" state ([source: `scanningDialog.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/scanningDialog.cpp)).
- **Lock delay**: `2.0f seconds` (`static constexpr float lock_delay = 2.0f;`). Science must hold all sliders within the threshold for **2 full seconds** before the round completes. The "LOCKED" label appears halfway through (at 1 second). If any slider drifts outside the threshold, the lock resets ([source: `scanningDialog.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/scanningDialog.h)).
- **After lock confirmed**: `scan_depth` advances by 1. If `scan_depth >= depth`, scan is complete. Otherwise, new target values are randomized and the next round begins.
- **Scan speed vs. range**: Range has **no effect** on scan speed. The time is purely how fast the player aligns sliders and holds them.
- **Analog joystick support**: Joystick axes can directly control slider positions (added in CHANGELOG) ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)).
- **Cancel button**: Present; can abort the scan at any time.

**Estimated total wall-clock time per round**: Slider setup + time to find target + 2-second hold. In practice, an experienced player takes ~5–15 seconds per round; novices might take 30+ seconds.

**Scan timing component** (`ScienceScanner`): Also has a `max_scanning_delay = 6.0f` field, suggesting a delay-based (rather than mini-game-based) scan variant exists in some configurations ([source: `scanning.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/scanning.h)).

### Mini-Game 2: Full Scan / Deep Scan (Second Scan / "FullScan")

**What Science learns (beyond SimpleScan):**
- **Shield frequency graph**: A bar chart showing all 21 frequency bands (0–20, displayed as 400–800 THz in 20 THz steps) colored green-to-red to show how effective each beam frequency would be against the target's shields. The "purest green" bar = maximum damage ([Science Console Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tz_6vXMQf-U); [source: `frequencyCurve.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/frequencyCurve.cpp)).
- **Beam frequency graph**: Shows which frequency the target's beams are at, and how effective each shield frequency would be in absorbing them ([source: `scienceScreen.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/scienceScreen.cpp)).
- **System health per subsystem**: Full scan exposes the health percentage of all target subsystems ([official docs](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=3); [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)).
- **Firing arcs visible**: After a full scan, the target's beam weapon firing arcs become visible on Helms and Weapons screens automatically ([official docs](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=3)).

**Mini-game complexity**: At `SC_Normal`, the full scan uses **1 slider** per round (easier than the simple scan's 2 sliders). At `SC_Advanced`, the full scan uses **2 sliders** per round ([source: `scanningDialog.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/scanningDialog.cpp)).

**Frequency system details** (on servers with `use_beam_shield_frequencies` enabled):
- Beam and shield frequencies are integers 0–20 (`max_frequency = 20`), displayed as "400THz" through "800THz" ([source: `beamweapon.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/beamweapon.h)).
- The damage factor formula: `sinf(...)` of the difference, scaled — matched frequencies deal about **50% damage**, opposite frequencies deal up to ~**150% damage** (a sinusoidal curve). The graph shows this full curve so the player can pick the optimal frequency ([source: `beamweapon.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/beamweapon.cpp)).

**ScanState progression:**
- `NotScanned` → `SimpleScan` (after round 1) → `FullScan` (after round 2)
- Some objects may be configured with `allow_simple_scan = false` to skip directly to full scan
- Scan state is **per-faction** (your team's scans persist for your faction only) ([source: `scanning.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/scanning.h)).

### Probe Launching

- **Who launches**: The **Relay officer** launches probes. Science cannot launch probes directly but can use them ([official docs](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=3)).
- **Max stock**: Up to **8 probes** by default (`ScanProbeLauncher::max = 8`) ([source: `probe.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/probe.h)).
- **Probe lifetime**: **10 minutes** (600 seconds) — documented in the official station guide; probes cannot be retrieved after launch ([official docs](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=3); [Relay Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pwLJiPuYP94)).
- **Probe radar radius**: 5U fixed short-range radar around the probe's position ([ScanProbe legacy code comment](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/spaceObjects/scanProbe.cpp)).
- **Replenishment**: Only by docking at a friendly station.
- **Probe-Science link**: Relay officer selects a deployed probe and presses "Link to Science." Science then sees the probe's 5U radar view and can scan ships within that radius — useful for scanning through nebulae or at extreme range ([official docs](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=3); [source: `relayScreen.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/relayScreen.cpp)).

### Nebulae and Jamming Effects

- **Nebulae**: Block long-range sensors. Entering a nebula drops effective range to short-range (~5U) in all directions. Nebulae are useful for hiding ([Science Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tz_6vXMQf-U)).
- **WarpJammer**: A scriptable station object that jams warp/jump drives for other factions within 5U. Does not affect sensors directly but forces tactical repositioning ([official docs](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=4)).
- **No explicit sensor jamming**: EE does not have a dedicated electronic countermeasures (ECM) mechanic natively. Nebulae are the primary "jamming" analog.

## Relay / Operations Station

Relay handles comms, probes, waypoints, and hacking. **Operations** is a 3-4 player variant that combines Science's scanning display with Relay's comms capability; it cannot initiate scans but shares the hacking function ([official docs](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=2)).

### Mini-Game 1: Hacking

**Overview**: The Relay (or Operations) officer can hack any non-friendly ship or station that is within short-range radar coverage of a friendly vessel ([source: `relayScreen.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/relayScreen.cpp)).

**Range / Line-of-Sight Requirement:**
- The target must be within **short-range radar distance (~5U)** of *any* friendly ship or station (not just the player's ship). The code checks `ShareShortRangeRadar` components across all friendly objects ([source: `relayScreen.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/relayScreen.cpp), lines 252–270).
- If the target moves out of this range, the hacking dialog **closes automatically** and the target is deselected.
- `HackingDevice` component must be present on the player ship (toggleable with `canHack()`) ([CHANGELOG 2020-04-09](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)).
- No explicit comms link required (hacking and comms are separate UI elements) ([source: `relayScreen.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/relayScreen.cpp)).

**Selecting a Target System:**
- Relay selects a ship on the map, then clicks "Start Hacking."
- A sidebar panel labeled "Target system" opens showing a **list of all hackable subsystems** on the target (those with `can_be_hacked = true`). Hackable systems include: Reactor, Beam Weapons, Missile System, Maneuvering, Impulse Engines, Warp Drive, Jump Drive, Front Shield Generator, Rear Shield Generator ([source: `shipsystem.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/shipsystem.h); [hackingDialog.cpp](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/hackingDialog.cpp)).
- The player selects a system; the mini-game begins immediately.

**The Mini-Game Itself (two variants, randomly chosen or server-configured):**

The server setting `hacking_games` selects which mini-game(s) are available: `HG_Lights` (Lights Out only), `HG_Mine` (Minesweeper only), or `HG_All` (random choice each attempt) ([source: `hackingDialog.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/hackingDialog.cpp)).

**Variant A — Lights Out** ([source: `lightsOut.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/lightsOut.cpp)):
- **Grid size**: `difficulty * 2 + 3` cells per side. Default `difficulty = 2` → **7×7 grid** (49 cells). Difficulty 1 → 5×5; Difficulty 3 → 9×9.
- **UI**: A square grid of toggle buttons. Each cell is either lit (on) or dark (off). All cells start lit. A random number of "moves" (3 to 3×grid_size) are applied to scramble the board.
- **Interaction**: Click any cell to toggle its state AND the state of all 4 orthogonally adjacent neighbors (classic Lights Out rules).
- **Goal**: Get ALL cells illuminated simultaneously.
- **Success condition**: `lights_on == grid_size * grid_size` — all cells lit.
- **Failure**: None — no failure state (unlimited moves). Player can press Reset to start a new scramble if stuck.
- **Progress bar**: Shows proportion of lit cells.
- **In-game text**: "To successfully hack this system, you must fully illuminate all binary countermeasure nodes."

**Variant B — Minesweeper** ([source: `mineSweeper.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/mineSweeper.cpp)):
- **Grid size**: `difficulty * 2 + 6` per side. Default difficulty 2 → **10×10 grid** (100 cells). `bomb_count = difficulty * 2 + 6` → 10 mines at default.
- **UI**: Standard Minesweeper grid; unrevealed cells are buttons. A flag-mode toggle button allows flagging cells (right-click or tap).
- **Interaction**: Left-click reveals a cell. If it's a mine, it shows "X". If empty, shows adjacent mine count or cascades to reveal neighbors.
- **Win condition**: All non-mine cells revealed.
- **Failure/Loss**: 2 mines revealed triggers failure (`MAX_ATTEMPTS = 2`; one mine is allowed, the second ends the game) ([source: `mineSweeper.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/mineSweeper.h)).
- **Progress bar**: Shows `correct_count / (field_size² - bomb_count)`.
- **Reset**: Player can reset at any time to get a new board configuration.

**Auto-Reset & Result Notification:**
- On completion (win or lose), the mini-game is disabled and a **2-second countdown** begins (`auto_reset_time = 2.0f`) shown as a progress bar ([source: `hackingDialog.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/hackingDialog.h)).
- After 2 seconds: if success, `commandHackingFinished()` is sent to server; if fail, a new game is generated. Player can keep attempting the same system.
- The status label shows current hacked percentage: "{target}: hacked {percent}%".

**Effects per Subsystem Hacked:**
- `hacked_level` increases by `HackingDevice::effectiveness = 0.5f` per successful hack (so **2 successful hacks = 100% hacked**) ([source: `playerInfo.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/playerInfo.cpp); [source: `hacking.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/hacking.h)).
- `hacked_level` is clamped 0.0–1.0.
- **Power penalty formula**: `effective_power = max(0, power_level - hacked_level * 0.75)`. So at 50% hacked (one hack), a system at 100% power runs at ~62.5% power. At 100% hacked, it runs at 25% power even at maximum power setting. **Double-hacked systems run at 25% efficiency** ([source: `shipsystem.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/shipsystem.cpp)).
- This reduced effectiveness applies to **all system outputs**: reactor energy generation, shield strength/regen, engine speed, beam fire rate/damage, jump drive charge rate, missile tube cycling, etc.
- **Recovery**: `InternalRepairCrew::unhack_per_second = 0.007` — Engineering repair crews slowly reduce `hacked_level` while stationed in the affected room at **0.007 hacked_level per second** (~143 seconds to fully un-hack a system by one crew member) ([source: `internalrooms.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/internalrooms.h)).

**Hacking difficulty levels** (server-configurable `hacking_difficulty`): Default = 2. Higher difficulty increases grid size for both mini-games ([source: `hackingDialog.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/hackingDialog.cpp)).

### Other Relay Duties

**Comms (Hailing):**
- Relay opens a text comms window to any ship or station within range.
- Friendly ships respond to orders (attack, dock, etc.). Friendly stations offer services (reinforcements, resupply).
- Reputation points are spent for some station requests ([official docs](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=3)).
- Docked ships: Relay requests missile rearmament from the station.
- Comms are **text-only** in the base game ("long range radio transceiver is in disrepair") — responses are from pre-scripted option menus ([Relay Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pwLJiPuYP94)).
- Operations station also has full comms access.

**Waypoints:**
- Relay clicks "Place Waypoint" anywhere on the sector map. Numbered waypoints appear.
- Waypoints are visible on Helms, Weapons, and Science radar displays as directional indicators (bearing visible, but **only Relay knows the distance** — requires verbal communication) ([Relay Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pwLJiPuYP94)).
- Waypoints are required for requesting aid from friendly stations.

**Science Database:**
- Both Science and Operations have access to an in-game database of all ship classes, weapons, and hazards.
- Useful for assessing capabilities before a full scan or identifying anomalies.

**Fleet Ally Calls:**
- Via comms with friendly stations: request backup or supply ships (costs reputation).
- Relay also manages alert level changes (affects UI overlays on all stations).

## Engineering Station

Engineering manages power, heat, coolant, and physical repairs. The **Engineering+** variant adds shield activation control (normally Weapons' job) and may display the ship interior map in larger format ([official docs](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=2)).

### Mini-Game 1: Power Slider Management

**The system matrix** ([source: `engineeringScreen.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/engineeringScreen.cpp); [Engineering Training v2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fxl6LpYIv-M)):
- A table with one row per ship system (Reactor, Beam Weapons, Missile System, Maneuvering, Impulse, Warp Drive, Jump Drive, Front Shield, Rear Shield = **9 systems** by default).
- Each row displays: **Health** (damage %), **Heat** level bar with trend arrow, **Power** level, **Coolant** level.
- Clicking a system row selects it; the large sliders at the right side activate for fine control.

**Power Slider:**
- Range: **0.0 to 3.0** (0% to 300% in the UI) ([source: `engineeringScreen.cpp` line 93](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/engineeringScreen.cpp)).
- Snap points at: 0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 (with extra-strong snap at 1.0) ([source: `engineeringScreen.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/engineeringScreen.cpp)).
- Default = 1.0 (100% power).
- **Effects of power level**:
  - **Reactor** (power UP): Generates more energy for the ship's energy pool. Energy draw actually *decreases* for the reactor when overpowered.
  - **Other systems** (power UP): Increases system output AND increases energy drain AND increases heat generation.
  - **Power DOWN** (<100%): Reduces output, reduces energy drain, reduces heat — a useful tactic to free up coolant.

**Heat Generation (the core balancing act):**
- Heat delta formula: `pow(1.7f, power_level - 1.0f) - (1.01f + coolant_level * 0.1f)` ([source: `shipsystem.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/shipsystem.h)).
- At 100% power, 0 coolant: `1.7^0 - 1.01 = 1.0 - 1.01 = -0.01` → very slowly cooling.
- At 200% power, 0 coolant: `1.7^1 - 1.01 = 1.7 - 1.01 = +0.69` → rapidly heating.
- At 300% power, 0 coolant: `1.7^2 - 1.01 = 2.89 - 1.01 = +1.88` → extremely rapid heating.
- Each unit of coolant removes `0.1` from the heating delta.
- Heat level range: **0.0 to 1.0**. System **overheats at 1.0** (heat_level cannot exceed 1.0; overflow is applied as damage).
- The **arrow indicator** in the heat column points up (heating) or down (cooling); brightness indicates the rate.
- An **overheat warning icon** flashes when heat > 0.9 ([source: `engineeringScreen.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/engineeringScreen.cpp)).

**Heat → Damage cascade** ([source: `shipsystem.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/shipsystem.cpp)):
- When heat exceeds 1.0 in one tick, the overflow is converted to health damage:
  - `health -= (overheat / heat_add_rate_per_second) * damage_per_second_on_overheat`
  - Default: `damage_per_second_on_overheat = 0.08f` (8% health lost per second of sustained overheat, at default heat_add_rate).
- Health scale: **-1.0 to 1.0**. Negative health = below 0% → system is inoperable.
- Systems with damaged health function as if underpowered: `effectiveness = max(0, power * health)`.

### Mini-Game 2: Coolant Allocation

**Total Coolant Pool:**
- Default max: **10.0 units** total (`Coolant::max = 10.0f`) ([source: `coolant.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/coolant.h)).
- Each system can hold up to **10.0 coolant** individually (`max_coolant_per_system = 10.0f`), but the ship's total pool limits the aggregate.
- Scriptable: `setMaxCoolant()` added in 2019 CHANGELOG ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)).

**Coolant Slider:**
- Range: 0.0 to 10.0 per system ([source: `engineeringScreen.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/engineeringScreen.cpp)).
- Snap points at 0, 2.5, 5, 7.5, 10.
- A **coolant remaining bar** at the top of the system list shows how much unallocated coolant exists. Dragging this slider forces proportional redistribution across all systems.

**Coolant heat reduction**: Each unit of coolant reduces the heating delta by 0.1. With 10 coolant on a system at 200% power: `0.69 - 1.0 = -0.31` (cooling, not heating).

**Auto-Coolant**: `Coolant::auto_levels = false` by default; can be enabled to let the server auto-distribute coolant. When enabled, manual coolant sliders are disabled ([source: `engineeringScreen.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/engineeringScreen.cpp)).

**The balancing loop** (the core gameplay tension):
1. Power a system up → heat rises → must add coolant
2. Adding coolant to system A → less coolant for system B
3. System B overheats → takes damage → must send repair crew
4. Repair crew in B's room → not repairing A if A is also damaged

### Mini-Game 3: Repair Crew (Engineering+ Interior Grid)

**The ship interior view** is part of Engineering (and Engineering+) screens, showing a top-down grid floor plan of the ship ([source: `shipInternalView.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screenComponents/shipInternalView.cpp); [Engineering Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fxl6LpYIv-M)).

**Rooms and Systems:**
- The ship is divided into rooms defined by `InternalRooms` component. Each room has a position and size (in grid cells), and is associated with a `ShipSystem::Type` ([source: `internalrooms.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/internalrooms.h)).
- Rooms are connected by doors; crew must navigate through doors to move between rooms.
- Room color shows system health: full health = white, damaged = tinted red.

**Repair Crew (InternalCrew):**
- Crew members are represented as small animated sprites on the floor plan.
- Each crew member has `move_speed = 2.0f` (grid cells per second) ([source: `internalrooms.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/internalrooms.h)).
- Engineering selects a crew member (click to select), then clicks a destination room (or uses keyboard arrows to move).
- The crew member pathfinds through doors to reach the target room.

**Repair Speed:**
- `InternalRepairCrew::repair_per_second = 0.007` — each repair crew member heals **0.007 health points per second** while in the damaged system's room ([source: `internalrooms.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/internalrooms.h)).
- At this rate: restoring a system from 0% to 100% health (1.0 health point) takes **~143 seconds** (~2 min 23 sec) with one crew member.
- Multiple crew in the same room stack their repair rates (additive).
- "Red zones" on the map indicate damaged systems, guiding crew dispatch ([Engineering Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fxl6LpYIv-M)).
- Hull repairs also occur when crew are present, but at a slower rate.

**Un-Hacking via Repair Crew:**
- `InternalRepairCrew::unhack_per_second = 0.007` — crew in a hacked system's room also reduce `hacked_level` at the same rate ([source: `internalrooms.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/internalrooms.h)).
- Added in the 2019-10-28 changelog: "Engineering can mitigate and repair hacking" ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)).

**Docked repairs**: While docked at a station, repair speed increases (mentioned in official docs, exact multiplier varies).

### Other Engineering Functions

**Self-Destruct Button:**
- Located top-left of the Engineering screen. Requires confirmation + code entry to activate ([Engineering Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fxl6LpYIv-M); [source: `engineeringScreen.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/engineeringScreen.cpp)).
- `SelfDestruct` component must be present on the ship (`canSelfDestruct()`).

**Shield Frequency:**
- Shield frequency selection is on the **Weapons station** (moved from Engineering — noted in CHANGELOG: "Shield frequency configuration moved from Engineering station to Weapons") ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md); [source: `weaponsScreen.cpp`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/screens/crew6/weaponsScreen.cpp)).
- **Recalibration downtime**: Changing shield frequency brings shields offline for **25 seconds** (`calibration_time = 25.0f`) ([source: `shields.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/shields.h)).
- Engineering+ station also has shield activation toggle.

**Beam Frequency:**
- Set by the **Weapons officer** via a frequency selector dial (instantaneous, no downtime) ([official docs](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/#tabs=3)).

**Jump Drive Charge-Up:**
- `JumpDrive::activation_delay = 10.0f` seconds — 10 seconds between pressing "Jump" and the actual jump occurring ([source: `jumpdrive.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/jumpdrive.h)).
- `charge_time = 90.0f` — after a maximum-range jump, the drive takes up to 90 seconds to recharge.
- Charge rate scales with `getSystemEffectiveness()` (power level × health, minus hacking): `recharge_rate = Tween::linear(effectiveness, 0, 1, -0.25, 1.0)`.
- At 0% effectiveness (e.g., fully hacked), the charge rate is **negative** (drive slowly loses charge).
- `energy_per_km_charge = 2.0` energy units per km of jump distance.
- `heat_per_jump = 0.35f` — a substantial heat spike on the jump drive system after each jump ([source: `jumpdrive.h`](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/blob/master/src/components/jumpdrive.h)).

## Cross-Station Verbal Communication Dependencies

These inter-station interactions **require verbal communication** — no automatic UI bridge exists:

1. **Science → Weapons**: "Their shields are weakest at frequency 12 [580 THz]. Set your beams there." (After full scan; frequency graphs are Science-side only)
2. **Science → Weapons**: "Your shields should match frequency 7 [540 THz] to block their beams." (After full scan)
3. **Science → Helms**: "Unknown contact bearing 047, range 18U — scan complete, it's hostile."
4. **Relay → Science**: "Launching probe to sector G7; link it to Science when it arrives." 
5. **Relay → Helms**: "Waypoint 3 is 14U ahead at bearing 045." (Only Relay sees distance on the map)
6. **Relay → All**: "Enemy ship's jump drive hacked — they can't escape. Also reactor at 25% effectiveness."
7. **Engineering → Captain/Weapons**: "Shields down for 25 seconds — recalibrating frequency, don't take hits."
8. **Engineering → All**: "Reactor at 200% power — may need to redistribute coolant. Alert if jump drive starts overheating."
9. **Captain → Engineering**: "Prioritize Impulse Engines — we need speed over weapons."

## Design Notes: Artemis SBS vs. EmptyEpsilon

EmptyEpsilon was created explicitly to improve on [Artemis Spaceship Bridge Simulator](http://artemis.eochu.com/). Key differences relevant to mini-game design:

- **Scanning**: Artemis uses a simpler 2-level scan system with no interactive mini-game; EE adds the slider alignment mechanic for active engagement.
- **Hacking**: Unique to EE; Artemis has no hacking station function. The minesweeper/lights-out implementation is acknowledged as a placeholder by lead developer Daid, with community discussion ongoing for improvements ([GitHub Issue #467](https://github.com/daid/EmptyEpsilon/issues/467)).
- **Engineering repair crew**: The ship interior grid with crew movement is EE-exclusive; Artemis Engineering manages power sliders only.
- **Relay probes**: EE probes have a strict 10-minute lifetime; Artemis probes are simpler. The probe-Science link creating a "remote scanner" is an EE innovation.
- **Comms**: EE comms are text-based branching menus (by design, not limitation), making the Relay role more active than Artemis's largely passive comms.
- **Open-source extensibility**: EE's Lua scripting allows custom mini-games, additional subsystems, and entirely new station functions.
