# Engineer score v2, 2026-10-05

KPI: `modules/ai/src/scoring/engineer-kpi.ts`. Validator: `npm --prefix modules/ai run score:engineer`. Head: `engineer_kpi30` of
`modules/ai/src/scoring/models/v3.json`. Previous report: `2026-10-03-engineer-kpi.md`. Jev spend $0 (no Jev call; every run is local).

## Result

- **Change:** the reserve is credited only in proportion to what the ship delivers (`R·min(1, S/0.3)`), and ε = 0. Before, K was
  `D60·((1−λ)S + λR)`: an all-shutdown ship (S ≈ 0) held a full store and scored the highest K on both E1 rungs and above idle on T1 and T1-MK2.
- **Ranking validity** (new check: Spearman r of the K gap with the graded-outcome gap over all 21 policy pairs): 0.43 → 0.67 on fit seeds 1-8,
  0.57 → 0.68 on held-out seeds 9-12, 0.53 → 0.72 (fit half) and 0.35 → 0.68 (held-out half) on fresh seeds 13-20.
- **idle > all-shutdown** now has a CI above 0 on every scenario, on both seed sets (before: ✗ on E1-MK2, E1-predator and T1-MK2, ✗ or spanning 0 on T1).
  reference > idle and reference > all-max keep their values (Δ ≤ 0.006 on every scenario).
- **The three weak spots as asked:** each rests on a flawed yardstick (next section). Not moved: pooled predictive validity (+0.001 → +0.023, within noise),
  the low tercile (−0.246 → −0.272), E1-predator reference − all-max (K Δ 0.015 → 0.015, CI spans 0; the graded outcome does not separate them either).
  **Cost:** frame-level within-moment validity on the E1 rungs falls (table below); that is the one thing this change makes worse.
- **Head v3:** the other six heads are bit-identical to v2. `engineer_kpi30` is relabelled and retrained: persistence gain 0.650 → 0.668 on the seed holdout, 0.82 on the fresh
  runs, calibration ECE 0.003 there. No regression against v1 or v2 on any head and scenario (CI above the noise floor of 0.002 logloss / 0.0005 mse on ≥ 5 runs).

## Why the yardsticks were flawed

1. **Pooled predictive validity** (the +0.001, the low tercile −0.246) correlates K with the outcome over every frame of every scenario at once. EVIDENCE: inside E1-MK2 and E1-predator, the
   legacy outcome and master's K are negatively related in every tercile (r −0.79 to −0.45), so pooled r is positive only through differences of scale between scenarios and the stretch of the fight (the store
   drains while damage lands later). It measures where a frame sits in a fight, not what the engineer did. The legacy numbers stay in the tables so before and after remain comparable.
2. **The E1 gate (damage per exposure second)** favours the ship that stays exposed longest. EVIDENCE: in E1-predator all-max ends the run at mean integrity 0.45 and reference at 0.17, yet reference has
   the lower per-second rate because it stays in range longer. The **graded outcome** (hostile integrity dealt minus own integrity lost; a kill counts 1) rates reference − all-max on E1-predator at −0.077 [−0.148, 0.007]
   and reference − idle at −0.102 [−0.223, 0.012]: no separation, if anything the other way. So "K should separate reference from all-max on E1-predator" is not supported by the game.
3. **Policy-ranking validity** replaces them: does K order the engineers the way the game does, over 21 pairs × seeds, with a tie band (|graded gap| < 0.02 dropped) and a CI over seeds.

## What could be wrong

- The graded outcome (hostile integrity dealt minus own integrity lost, first-frame hostiles) is my yardstick, not a game rule. Both K forms are judged by it, so a bias in it could favour the new K. Under the gate's own
  outcomes the required contrasts keep their sign and CI, so the change does not depend on it for the existing orderings.
- E1 frame-level validity falls (above). If E1 matters more than the pooled and run-level checks, this change is a trade.
- `S0` = 0.3 is a constant I chose. Results are identical for 0.1-0.3; ranking validity is slightly lower at 0.8 (fit 0.69 against 0.70).
- The scripted reference engineer is the only "good" policy. Ranking validity measures agreement with the game's outcome over seven scripted policies, not over Jev or human engineers (INFERENCE: it may differ there).
- Sample sizes: 12 + 8 seeds per scenario. The held-out halves (4 and 2 seeds) have wide CIs, E1-MK2 and T1-MK2 most.
- Validator caches `*.ekpi5.json` were built after rebuilding core; a first set built against a stale core build (blastRate 0) was discarded.
